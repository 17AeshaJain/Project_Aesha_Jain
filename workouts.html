Schedule

Beginner Weekly Routine

A balanced 7-day plan mixing strength, cardio, and recovery. Pick either the home or gym track for the workout days.

Day Focus Duration Key Exercises Intensity
Monday Full Body Strength 30–40 min Squats · Push-ups · Rows · Plank Moderate
Tuesday Cardio + Core 25–35 min Jumping Jacks · High Knees · Crunches · Leg Raises Light
Wednesday Upper Body 30–40 min Push-ups · Dumbbell Press · Tricep Dips · Shoulder Taps Moderate
Thursday Active Recovery / Yoga 20–30 min Light stretching · Yoga flow · Walk Easy
Friday Lower Body 35–45 min Lunges · Glute Bridges · Wall Sit · Calf Raises Moderate
Saturday Full Body HIIT 20–30 min Burpees · Mountain Climbers · Jump Squats · Plank High
Sunday Complete Rest — Sleep, eat well, hydrate, relax Rest

⚠️ Warm Up Always

Spend 5 minutes doing light jogging in place, arm circles, and hip rotations before every session. This prevents injury and improves performance.

🧊 Cool Down Matters

End every workout with 5 minutes of static stretching. Hold each stretch 20–30 seconds. Your future self will thank you.

📈 Progressive Overload

Add 1–2 reps or a slightly harder variation each week. Consistent small improvements compound into massive results over months.

😴 Rest = Growth

Muscles grow during recovery, not during the workout itself. Never skip rest days — they are part of the program, not a sign of weakness.

Basics

Fitness Fundamentals

Master these concepts and you'll understand more than 90% of gym beginners.

🔄
Sets & Reps

A "rep" is one complete movement. A "set" is a group of reps. Beginners typically start with 3 sets of 8–12 reps per exercise.

⏱️
Rest Periods

Rest 60–90 seconds between sets for strength. 30–45 seconds for cardio/endurance. Rest is productive — use it intentionally.

📐
Form First

Always prioritise correct form over weight or speed. Bad form = injury. Record yourself or use a mirror to self-correct.

🫀
Heart Rate Zones

Zone 2 (60–70% max HR) burns fat. Zone 4 (80–90%) builds cardio fitness. Start with Zone 2 to build base aerobic capacity.

🏆
Consistency Wins

3 mediocre workouts a week beats 1 perfect session. Show up. Results come from weeks and months of consistent effort.

📓
Track Your Progress

Write down your workouts — exercises, sets, reps, weight. Seeing progress on paper is one of the strongest motivators.

3–4 days per week is ideal for beginners. This gives you enough stimulus to make progress while allowing adequate recovery between sessions. Start with 3 and add a 4th day after 4–6 weeks once you feel comfortable.

For most goals, do weights first, then cardio. Strength training requires more neuromuscular coordination and energy — doing it fatigued after cardio reduces effectiveness and increases injury risk. If you're training primarily for cardio fitness, flip the order.

You'll feel better within 2 weeks (energy, mood, sleep). Visible physical changes typically take 4–8 weeks of consistent training and good nutrition. Strength gains happen faster — you'll notice you can lift more or do more reps within 2–3 weeks.
