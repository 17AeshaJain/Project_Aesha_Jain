🏋️ GYM WORKOUTS

First time at the gym? These beginner plans use machines and free weights safely. We keep it simple, effective, and ego-free — leave the heavy lifting for later.

Before You Start

Gym Etiquette & Safety

Knowing how to behave at the gym builds confidence from day one. These unwritten rules matter.

🧹 Re-rack Your Weights

Always return dumbbells and plates to where you found them. This is the number one gym rule — no exceptions.

🧴 Wipe Down Equipment

Use the gym's spray and paper towels before and after every machine or bench you use. Common courtesy.

🤫 Limit Phone Time

Between sets is fine. But don't sit on a machine scrolling for 10 minutes while others wait. Be aware of your surroundings.

🙋 Ask for Help

Never guess with barbells or unfamiliar machines. Gym staff are there for exactly this. Ask — no one will judge you.

🪞 Mirror = Form, Not Selfies

Gym mirrors help you check your form. Use them for that purpose — record a quick set to review technique later.

📉 Start Light

Ego lifting causes injuries. Start with a weight where you can do 12 clean reps. Progress from there over weeks.

Workout A

Push Day — Chest · Shoulders · Triceps

3 sets × 10–12 reps each. Rest 60–90 seconds between sets. Choose a weight where the last 2 reps are challenging.

01

Machine Chest Press

Adjust seat so handles align with mid-chest · Press forward fully · Return slowly (2 seconds back). Perfect first chest machine for beginners — safer than bench press.

3×12
02

Dumbbell Incline Press

Bench at 30–45° incline · Press dumbbells from shoulder height · Extend fully without locking elbows · Lower with control. Targets upper chest and front deltoids.

3×10
03

Dumbbell Lateral Raise

Stand with dumbbells at sides · Raise arms out to shoulder height · Lead with elbows · Lower controlled. Builds width through the shoulders — a game-changer for your physique.

3×12
04

Cable Front Raise

Attach handle to low pulley · Stand facing away · Raise arm to shoulder height · Lower slowly. Isolates the front deltoid with constant cable tension.

3×12/arm
05

Cable Tricep Pushdown

Attach rope or bar to high pulley · Elbows pinned to sides · Push down until arms fully extended · Return slowly. The best beginner isolation for triceps.

3×12
06

Machine Pec Fly (Pec Deck)

Adjust so arms are bent at 90° · Bring handles together in a wide arc in front of chest · Feel the chest stretch on the way back. Finisher for chest — great pump.

3×15
07

Overhead Dumbbell Extension

Sit on bench · Hold one dumbbell overhead with both hands · Lower behind head by bending elbows · Press back up. Long-head tricep isolation — arms look great from behind.

3×12
08

Dumbbell Arnold Press

Start with dumbbells at shoulder height, palms facing you · Rotate palms outward as you press overhead · Reverse on the way down. Hits all three heads of the shoulder simultaneously.

3×10

Workout B

Pull Day — Back · Biceps · Rear Delts

3 sets × 10–12 reps. Focus on feeling the target muscle work — not just moving the weight.

01

Lat Pulldown (Machine)

Grip bar slightly wider than shoulder width · Pull to upper chest · Lean back slightly · Return slowly. Best beginner substitute for pull-ups — builds the same muscles.

3×12
02

Seated Cable Row

Sit upright · Pull handle to abdomen · Squeeze shoulder blades together · Return with control · Don't use momentum. Thickness in the mid-back — critical for posture.

3×12
03

Dumbbell Single-Arm Row

Brace one hand and knee on bench · Row dumbbell to hip · Elbow stays close to body · Lower with control. One of the best exercises for building a wide, strong back.

3×10/arm
04

Face Pull (Cable)

Set cable at face height with rope · Pull rope toward face · Flare elbows out · Hold 1 second · Return. Essential for shoulder health and rear delt development.

3×15
05

Dumbbell Bicep Curl

Stand with dumbbells at sides · Curl up with palms facing up · Squeeze at top · Lower fully · Don't swing! The classic. Supinate your wrists at the top for full activation.

3×12
06

Hammer Curl

Neutral grip (thumbs up) · Curl up without rotating wrist · Lower slowly. Targets the brachialis — the muscle that pushes your bicep peak up visually.

3×12
07

Wide-Grip Lat Pulldown

Take a wider grip than shoulder-width · Pull down to collarbone · Focus on pulling your elbows into your back pockets · Slow, controlled release. Great for width.

3×10
08

Cable Bicep Curl

Low pulley with straight bar or EZ bar · Stand back slightly · Curl up with constant tension throughout · Lower fully. Cable maintains tension at the bottom unlike dumbbells.

3×15

Workout C

Leg Day — Quads · Hamstrings · Glutes · Calves

Don't skip leg day. Ever. 3–4 sets × 10–15 reps. Legs are your biggest muscle group — training them raises your entire body's metabolism.

01

Goblet Squat (Dumbbell)

Hold one dumbbell vertically at chest · Squat deep with elbows inside knees · Drive up through heels. Best intro squat for beginners — the weight forces an upright torso naturally.

3×12
02

Leg Press (Machine)

Feet shoulder-width on platform · Lower slowly until 90° knee bend · Press up without locking knees · Control descent. Safe first heavy leg exercise — learn leg drive here.

3×12
03

Leg Extension (Machine)

Sit with pad across shins · Extend legs fully · Squeeze quads at top · Lower slowly. Perfect quad isolation — use lighter weight and feel it burn.

3×15
04

Leg Curl (Machine)

Lie prone · Pad behind ankles · Curl legs toward glutes · Lower slowly. Directly targets the hamstrings — the most neglected beginner muscle group.

3×12
05

Romanian Deadlift (Dumbbell)

Stand holding dumbbells at hips · Hinge forward from hips · Lower weights along legs · Feel hamstrings stretch · Drive hips forward to stand. Best beginner hip hinge pattern.

3×10
06

Walking Lunges (Dumbbell)

Hold light dumbbells at sides · Step forward and lower back knee toward floor · Push forward leg to step into next lunge. Unilateral training that corrects muscle imbalances.

3×10/leg
07

Standing Calf Raise

Stand on edge of a step · Lower heels below step · Drive up onto toes · Hold 1 second at top · Lower slowly. Calves respond to high reps — go for the burn here.

4×20
08

Hip Abductor Machine

Sit with pads on outer thighs · Push legs apart against resistance · Return controlled. Targets glute medius — crucial for knee health and hip stability.

3×15
09

Hip Adductor Machine

Sit with pads on inner thighs · Push legs together · Control the return. Often overlooked — balances the hip abductors and stabilises the pelvis during squats.

3×15
10

Barbell Back Squat (Beginner)

Bar rests on upper traps · Grip just outside shoulders · Feet hip-width · Squat until thighs are parallel · Drive up. The ultimate lower body compound exercise — start with just the bar!

3×8

Bonus

Gym Core Finisher (10 min)

Add this to the end of any workout, 3× per week. Core work done well transforms your stability and posture.

💪
Cable Crunch

Kneel facing pulley · Rope behind head · Crunch down contracting abs · 3×15. Loaded core flexion with adjustable resistance.

🔄
Cable Pallof Press

Stand sideways to cable · Press handle straight out · Hold 2 seconds · Return. Anti-rotation — builds the deep core stability nobody talks about.

📐
Ab Wheel Rollout

Start on knees · Roll wheel forward until nearly flat · Pull back · 3×8. Advanced but incredibly effective once you have baseline core strength.

⬆️
Hanging Knee Raise

Hang from pull-up bar · Draw knees to chest · Lower slowly · 3×10. Decompresses the spine and works the lower abs and hip flexors.

🧱
Plank with Shoulder Tap

High plank · Touch opposite shoulder with one hand while staying stable · 3×20 taps. Adds anti-rotation challenge to an already great exercise.

🌊
Russian Twist (Plate)

Sit leaning back at 45° · Hold plate · Rotate side to side · 3×20. Oblique training with added load — builds that athletic V-taper waist.

Sample Split

3-Day Beginner Gym Split

Ideal schedule if you can hit the gym 3 days per week. Rest or do light cardio on off days.

Day Session Focus Duration
Monday Workout A — Push Chest · Shoulders · Triceps 50–60 min
Tuesday Rest / Walk Light active recovery 20–30 min walk
Wednesday Workout B — Pull Back · Biceps · Rear Delts 50–60 min
Thursday Rest / Yoga Stretch & recover 20 min yoga
Friday Workout C — Legs Quads · Hamstrings · Glutes · Calves 55–65 min
Saturday Optional: Core Core finisher routine 10–15 min
Sunday Full Rest Sleep, eat, recover —

💡 Pro tip: After 8 weeks on the 3-day split, consider upgrading to a 4-day Upper/Lower split for more volume and faster progress. Your body adapts — keep challenging it.

Long-Term Success

Progressive Overload Explained

The single most important concept in gym training. Without this, you stop making progress.

📈 Add Reps

Doing 3×10 comfortably? Try 3×11 or 3×12 next session. Once you hit the top of your rep range, it's time to increase weight.

⬆️ Add Weight

When you can hit 12 clean reps, add the smallest increment (usually 2.5kg or 5kg). Small jumps over time = massive total gains.

🔁 Add Sets

Increase from 3 sets to 4 sets before increasing weight. More volume = more growth stimulus, especially for hypertrophy goals.

⏱️ Reduce Rest Time

Doing 90s rest between sets? Drop to 75s. Same workout, more compressed. Increases intensity and cardio demand simultaneously.
